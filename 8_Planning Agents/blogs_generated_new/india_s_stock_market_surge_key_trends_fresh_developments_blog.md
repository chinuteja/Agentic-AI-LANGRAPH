# Recent Developments Shaping the Indian Stock Market – July 2026 Update

## Market Performance & Sector Highlights  

July 2026 marked a **strong recovery** for Indian equities, delivering a **second consecutive month of gains**.  As highlighted in the LinkedIn market review, the rally was accompanied by **heightened volatility**, driven largely by macro‑economic data releases and global risk‑off sentiment【1】.  

### IT sector leads the charge  
- The **Nifty IT index surged >3%**, outpacing the broader market and cementing information technology as the standout sector for the month【1】.  
- **Infosys** and **TCS** were the primary contributors, posting double‑digit percentage gains and reinforcing their status as blue‑chip growth engines【2】.  

### Other notable movers  

| Category | Stock | Performance (July 2026) |
|----------|-------|--------------------------|
| **Top gainers** | **Bajaj Finance** | +≈7% (driven by strong loan book growth)【2】 |
| **Losers** | **Indian Railway Catering & Tourism Corp. (IRCTC)** | –≈5% (profit warning and regulatory concerns)【2】 |

The Facebook “Top Moving Stocks” post for the week of **July 6‑10, 2026** corroborates these moves, listing Infosys, TCS, Bajaj Finance among the week’s biggest winners and IRCTC as a prominent laggard【2】.  

Overall, July’s market dynamics underscore a **resilient recovery** tempered by **volatile trading conditions**, with the IT sector and select financial stocks driving the upside while a few high‑profile names lagged behind.

## Regulatory & Policy Changes  

| Change | What’s new | Potential market impact |
|--------|------------|--------------------------|
| **RBI amendment – Fully Accessible Route (FAR) for FPIs in government securities** | The Reserve Bank of India has broadened the *Fully Accessible Route* (FAR) for Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs), allowing them to invest directly in Indian government securities without the need for a domestic custodian or a designated depository participant. The amendment also relaxes the eligibility criteria for FPIs, streamlining the onboarding process and expanding the pool of eligible foreign investors【https://www.tempuslaw.co.in/post/understanding-key-amendments-july-2026】. | • **Higher foreign inflows** – Easier access is expected to attract more FPI capital, supporting demand for sovereign bonds and potentially lowering yields.<br>• **Liquidity boost** – Direct participation can deepen the secondary market for government securities, reducing bid‑ask spreads.<br>• **Currency dynamics** – Increased foreign demand may provide short‑term support to the rupee, especially during periods of heightened volatility. |
| **SEBI’s proposed reforms to the Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) framework** | The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has issued a draft framework that shifts the responsibility for handling investor‑broker disputes from the Securities Appellate Tribunal to the market‑infrastructure institutions (e.g., stock exchanges, clearing corporations). The new ODR mechanism will be technology‑driven, offering a faster, cost‑effective, and transparent resolution process【https://synergialegal.com/legal-briefs-bi-weekly-legal-updates-volume-2-issue-20-july-13-2026-july-25-2026】. | • **Improved investor confidence** – Faster dispute settlement reduces litigation risk, encouraging both domestic and foreign investors to trade more actively.<br>• **Operational efficiency** – Exchanges and clearing houses will need to upgrade their tech platforms, creating ancillary business opportunities for fintech providers.<br>• **Regulatory clarity** – A standardized ODR process can lower compliance costs for brokerage firms and may lead to tighter adherence to market‑conduct norms. |

### Why these updates matter now  

- **Capital inflows are a key driver of market breadth.** By simplifying the FAR, the RBI is positioning India’s sovereign debt market to capture a larger share of the global “safe‑haven” search, especially as major economies grapple with tightening monetary policies.  
- **Investor protection is increasingly a competitive edge.** SEBI’s ODR overhaul aligns India with global best practices (e.g., EU’s ODR platform), signaling a commitment to transparent dispute handling—a factor that can tip the scale for institutional investors weighing emerging‑market exposure.  

Together, these regulatory shifts are likely to **enhance market depth, improve liquidity, and bolster confidence**, setting the stage for a more resilient Indian equity and debt ecosystem in the second half of 2026.
